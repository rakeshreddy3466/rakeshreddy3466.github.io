Concept project, 2026

Should the network engineer trust this suggestion

As radio networks get more automated, more decisions start as an AI suggestion. If the screen hides the reasoning, engineers either ignore the AI or follow it blindly. I explored how an operations engineer can see why a suggestion was made, judge the risk, and approve, edit or reject it in seconds.

Role
Research, interaction design, prototyping
Deliverables
Journey map, sitemap, user flows, wireframes, Figma prototype
Test plan
Think aloud sessions comparing a summary and an evidence version, with the System Usability Scale and a trust questionnaire
Journey of an operations engineer receiving a suggestion
  1. 1
    NoticeThinks: is this urgent or can it wait. Design: one line that says what, when and how sure.
  2. 2
    QuestionThinks: why these cells and why tonight. Design: evidence one tap away, never forced on everyone.
  3. 3
    Judge the riskThinks: what breaks if the AI is wrong. Design: coverage risk and the undo plan shown before the buttons.
  4. 4
    DecideThinks: I want to adjust, not just accept. Design: approve, edit the window, or reject with a reason.
  5. 5
    Follow upThinks: did it work. Design: a morning summary of what was saved and whether anyone noticed.

How I work

  1. 1. Sit with the people who own the problemInterviews and workshops before any screen. I write down how the work really happens, not how the process says it does.
  2. 2. Map itJourney maps, sitemaps and user flows that the whole team can point at and argue about.
  3. 3. Prototype in Figma, then in codeWireframes first, then a working prototype when real data or real timing matters.
  4. 4. Test and iterateThink aloud sessions, simple measures like the System Usability Scale, and changes I can explain from what people did.
